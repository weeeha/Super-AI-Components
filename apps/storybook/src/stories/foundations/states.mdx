# States

A generation-aware component answers one question with its body: where is the run. The design
specification fixes the vocabulary for that answer as `idle`, `queued`, `streaming`, `done`,
`failed` and `locked`, and calls it the state contract
(`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-06-10-super-ai-components-design.md` section 6). This page covers how
those states paint here, which components add names of their own, and the focus and selection
treatments every control carries whether or not it generates anything. How a state change is timed
is **Foundations → Motion**.

## The contract names six; a control may name more

The six names live in one place, `apps/docs/registry/super-ai/cost.tsx` line 159, as the exported
`GenerationState` union. `result-card.tsx` line 38 takes that type verbatim rather than restating
it, which is why the card is the reference rendering of the full contract.

Components that own a narrower job declare their own union and say so in a header comment.
`run-button.tsx` lines 20 to 27 names seven: `idle`, `estimating`, `running`, `done`, `failed`,
`insufficient-credits` and `locked`. `generation-queue.tsx` line 40 names five per item: `queued`,
`running`, `done`, `failed` and `cancel`. `preview-tile.tsx` line 8 names four: `default`,
`loading`, `locked` and `failed`.

Those unions are not a divergence to tidy up. `result-card.tsx` lines 47 to 54 maps its six onto
the tile's four, sending both `queued` and `streaming` to the tile's single `loading`, and the
comment above the map records why: a narrower component should not grow a state it cannot draw
differently. Map at the boundary, name what you can paint.

## Failure renders where the result would have been

Design principle 6 in the specification is that failure is a first-class state rendered inline
where the result would have been, never only a toast. That is what the tile does: the failed
treatment fills the frame the picture was going to occupy, and the retry control lives inside it.

The failure text is `text-foreground`, not `text-destructive`, and the comment at
`preview-tile.tsx` line 119 carries the measurement: destructive on this frame's `bg-muted` reads
4.34:1 against a 4.5:1 minimum. Colour is never the only carrier of the failure anyway, so the
message says it. `run-button.tsx` line 232 does the same job in a line rather than a frame: a
`role="alert"` span pointed at by the trigger's `aria-describedby`.

## Locked replaces the trigger; disabled only dims it

They look adjacent and they are not. Disabled says the control is unavailable and offers nothing
else. Locked says the action exists behind a gate and offers the way through it, which is why
`run-button.tsx` line 126 swaps the whole trigger for an unlock button plus a reason, and
`media-prompt-bar.tsx` line 168 replaces the composer with a title, a description and a call to
action.

The credits shortfall is a third case again, and `run-button` keeps it separate from both:
`state="insufficient-credits"` renders an add-credits button beside the shortfall line, phrased
from the same `cost` and `balance` the chip was already using.

## Two focus recipes ship, and both are keyboard-only

Every interactive element in the registry pairs a suppressed outline with a visible replacement,
which is what `STA-3` requires. Measured across the 144 non-test registry sources on 2026-09-09,
the dominant recipe is `focus-visible:ring-ring focus-visible:ring-2
focus-visible:outline-none`, at 75, 75 and 74 sites. The second is the slider-thumb recipe,
`border-ring ring-ring/50 hover:ring-3 focus-visible:ring-3 active:ring-3`, at 11 sites across
`compare-viewer`, `parameter-panel`, `stem-mixer`, `time-ruler`, `tts-composer` and
`waveform-editor`. The vendored `components/ui/button.tsx` base carries a third,
`focus-visible:border-ring focus-visible:ring-3 focus-visible:ring-ring/50`, so anything built on
that button inherits it.

`STA-3` carries three exemptions, and each is a case where the outline is suppressed on something
that is not a tab stop. In `field-row.tsx` line 61 the bare input sits inside a wrapper that rings
on its behalf with `focus-within:ring-2`. In `generation-wizard.tsx` line 208 and
`onboarding-wizard.tsx` line 280 the suppressed element is a step heading with `tabIndex` of minus
one, focused so a step change is announced rather than to put it in the tab order.

Reading a ring back in a test is harder than it looks, and
`apps/storybook/src/lib/focus-ring.ts` records why: a Tailwind ring compiles to composed
`box-shadow` layers that are all present while the ring is off, at zero alpha and zero size, so a
check against the string `none` passes on an element painting nothing. Use `settledFocusRing` from
that module in a `KeyboardOrder` play function.

## Selected is a claim about the data; focus is a claim about the cursor

Selection is durable and lives on an ARIA attribute, so it survives losing focus and reaches
assistive technology without a colour. Counted on 2026-09-09: `aria-pressed` in 19 files,
`aria-current` in 13, `aria-checked` in 6, `aria-selected` in 4, `data-selected` in 2.
`preview-tile.tsx` line 47 makes the choice explicit through a `selectMode` prop: `"toggle"`
reports `aria-pressed`, and `"open"` omits it, because a tile that navigates makes no claim about
being pressed.

The two treatments stack rather than compete. `preview-tile.tsx` line 105 paints selection with
`ring-2 ring-offset-2` and the comment beside it gives the reason a ring rather than a border: a
ring is a `box-shadow`, contributes no layout, and so selecting a tile never reflows the grid.

## Busy is announced, not only repainted

A relabelled button is a repaint, and a screen reader is not reliably told. Registry components add
a live region: `role="status"` in 34 files, `aria-live` in 14, `aria-busy` in 6, all measured
2026-09-09. `run-button.tsx` line 239 is the pattern worth copying, an `sr-only` polite status
region holding the running or estimating label, alongside `aria-busy` on the wrapper.

The queue is the same contract at list scale, one state per row rather than one per control.

## Rules

- Take the vocabulary from `GenerationState` in `apps/docs/registry/super-ai/cost.tsx` when a
  component is generation-aware. Declare a narrower or wider union only when the component paints
  those names differently, and map at the boundary the way `result-card.tsx` maps six onto four.
- Render a failure inline, where the result would have been, and put the retry inside it. A toast
  alone loses the association with the thing that failed.
- Never carry a failure in colour alone, and never put `text-destructive` on the `bg-muted` frame:
  the pairing measures 4.34:1 against a 4.5:1 minimum, recorded in
  `docs/design-system/a11y-baseline.md`.
- Locked and disabled are different states. Locked replaces the trigger with the way through and
  names the gate; disabled dims a control and offers nothing. A credits shortfall is neither, and
  gets its own branch.
- Pair any suppressed outline with a visible `focus-visible` replacement, which is what `STA-3`
  requires. Suppress without one only where the element is not a tab stop, and say so at the site.
- Use `focus-visible`, never `focus`. The ring is for the keyboard.
- Carry selection on an ARIA attribute the reader can hear, and let the focus ring stack on top of
  it. Never spend the ring on selection, and never let selection suppress it.
- Announce a busy transition through a live region rather than relying on a relabelled control.
- Token names and the values behind `--ring` live in **Foundations → Design Tokens**; the contrast
  decisions behind them are in **Foundations → Inclusive design**.
