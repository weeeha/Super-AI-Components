# Data Visualization

This theme ships a five-step chart palette with no colour in it, which decides more about charting
here than any form rule would. This page reports what actually draws data in the registry, what the
palette measures, and the two chart rules that gate. Every value below was read from this repo on
2026-09-09. The numbers inside the examples are sample data.

## Five components draw data, and one of them uses a chart library

Measured by grep over `apps/docs/registry/`: `usage-dashboard.tsx` is the only registry source that
imports `recharts` (line 5) or the shadcn chart port at `@/components/ui/chart` (line 8). The others
draw with layout and text.

- `usage-dashboard`, a period selector driving a summary row and a per-model breakdown. The
  breakdown carries a bar chart.
- `quota-meter`, proportion bars built from elements rather than marks, with three states. It treats
  `near-limit` as a state of its own because the useful moment is before zero, and `over-limit` as a
  third, because a plan you have already exceeded is not one you are about to exceed
  (`quota-meter.tsx` lines 29 to 31).
- `credits-indicator`, the one hand-drawn glyph in this group: a single ring. Its `total` prop is
  required for the ring form, because a ring with no denominator is a circle (line 11).
- `trace-timeline`, a waterfall whose bars are positioned by start time on a shared axis rather than
  stacked. Overlapping spans keep their overlap, since hiding the collision is the failure a
  waterfall exists to prevent, and a retry is a new row rather than a replacement
  (`trace-timeline.tsx` lines 32 to 41).
- `stat-readout`, a definition list with no marks at all. An absent value renders a dash rather than
  a blank cell, so the absence reads as deliberate (line 36).

Reach for the chart port when the shape of a series is the point. Reach for the other four when a
figure, a proportion or an ordering is the point, and skip the library.

## The palette is five greys, and it is the same in both themes

`apps/storybook/src/index.css` declares `--chart-1` through `--chart-5` twice, at lines 107 to 111 in
`:root` and lines 142 to 146 in `.dark`, and the two blocks are identical. All five sit at chroma 0,
so they are neutral: lightness 0.87, 0.556, 0.439, 0.371 and 0.269, from `--chart-1` down to
`--chart-5`. The `@theme inline` block aliases them to `--color-chart-1` through `--color-chart-5`
at lines 58 to 62, which is what a Tailwind class or a `var()` in chart config reads.

Two consequences follow from the same file, and both are worth checking before you assign a series.

- The ramp does not invert between themes, so a step that reads as the emphasised series on a light
  page reads as the quiet one on a dark page. `--chart-1` at lightness 0.87 is near the light page
  background (1.0) and far from the dark one (0.145).
- The bottom of the ramp crowds the dark surfaces. `--chart-5` at 0.269 is the same lightness as
  dark `--muted`, `--accent` and `--secondary`, and sits a short step above dark `--card` at 0.205.
  A five-series chart on a dark card loses its darkest series into the card.

Three or four series is what this palette supports comfortably. Past that, split the chart.

## Emphasis is a decision, and nothing checks it

`CHT-1` in `packages/ds-rules/rules/core.json` carries the convention in its own fix text: the
series answering the question the card asks gets emphasis, and the context series go grey. No script
can tell which series that is, so the rule detects the palette and leaves the emphasis to the author.
With a neutral ramp, emphasis has to come from something other than hue: the position of the series
in the ramp, the ordering of the marks, or the sentence beside the chart.

## A chart is flat, and gridded on one axis

The two chart rules both gate. Both live in `core.json`, both are blockers, and `pnpm check:tokens`
runs them at blocker severity over `apps/docs/registry/super-ai` and `apps/docs/registry/marketing`.

- `CHT-1`, chart colors are `chart-1` to `chart-5`, never library defaults. It greps for the recharts
  default palette, which the record calls the clearest single tell of an unguided chart.
- `CHT-3`, flat marks and hairline gridlines, never the recharts dash default. It greps for the
  library default dash pattern.

`COL-1` reaches charts too, at blocker severity: no gradient fills, no glow, no third dimension.

## The numbers live in text beside the chart

`usage-dashboard` is worth reading as the worked example, because it decided the harder half. Its
per-model breakdown renders every figure as real table content, a `th` with `scope="row"` per model
and spend, tokens and latency as sibling cells in the same row. The chart beside that table is
marked `aria-hidden`, uses one fill, and turns off the recharts accessibility layer, which the file
justifies at lines 39 to 50: the chart shows nothing the table does not already say, and recharts
would otherwise put a focusable element inside an aria-hidden subtree, which is an axe violation by
construction.

That is the pattern to copy. A chart is a second reading of numbers that exist somewhere as text,
and a delta sits beside the figure it belongs to rather than below it or in a tooltip, because a
number without a direction is not actionable.

## A chart with no data says so in the same box

`usage-dashboard.tsx` line 284 branches on an empty model list and renders a sentence inside the
same card, so the panel keeps its place instead of vanishing. That covers one of the four states a
data panel can be in.

The other two, loading and failed, are not drawn by any chart-bearing component in this registry
today. That is a measurement rather than a recommendation: when you build a panel that fetches,
draw the wait and the failure in the box where the chart will land, and take the shapes from
**Patterns → Loading states** and **Content → Error messages**.

## Rules

- Use `--chart-1` to `--chart-5` for series colour, never a library default. Rule `CHT-1`, blocker,
  run by `pnpm check:tokens`.
- Gridlines are hairline, solid and on one axis. Rule `CHT-3`, blocker.
- No gradient, glow or three-dimensional mark. Rule `COL-1`, blocker.
- Keep a chart to three or four series in this theme. The palette is neutral and the bottom of it
  crowds the dark surfaces.
- Put every number the chart shows in text as well. A chart that is the only source of a figure is a
  chart a screen reader user cannot read.
- Draw the empty, loading and failed states in the box the chart occupies, at the chart's size.
- Sample data is something this system could really emit: generation counts, token counts, model
  names. No invented companies and no fabricated metrics, per
  `docs/design-system/story-conventions.md`.
