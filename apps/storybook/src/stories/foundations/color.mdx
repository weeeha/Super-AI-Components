# Color

This theme is neutral to an unusual degree, and that is measurable rather than a matter of taste.
Everything a component reaches for is a grey, so hierarchy has to come from lightness, edges and
placement. The page below says which greys, which pairs are safe, and which pair has failed the
accessibility gate five separate rounds.

## Only three declarations carry any colour

Measured 2026-09-09 across both blocks of `apps/storybook/src/index.css`: of the 62 colour
declarations, 59 carry zero chroma, two of those as a white alpha (the dark `--border` and
`--input`). The three exceptions are `--destructive` in light (`oklch(0.577 0.245 27.325)`),
`--destructive` in dark (`oklch(0.704 0.191 22.216)`), and `--sidebar-primary` in dark
(`oklch(0.488 0.243 264.376)`, a saturated blue at hue 264, whose light counterpart is a neutral).

So there is no accent hue in this theme. `--primary` is near-black in light and near-white in dark,
and it inverts with the ground rather than sitting on a hue. Anything that looks coloured in a
composition is either destructive, that one sidebar token, or a `--marketing-*` property.

## Surfaces: six tokens, two values

In light mode `--background`, `--card` and `--popover` are all pure white, and `--muted`,
`--accent` and `--secondary` are all `oklch(0.97 0 0)`. A fourth token joins the second group,
`--sidebar-accent`, so four names resolve to one value.

Two things follow. A card reads by its border in light mode, not by its fill, so removing the
border removes the card. And nesting muted inside muted inside secondary buys nothing, because all
three are one value. Dark mode does separate the page from the raised surfaces, `0.145` under
`0.205`, with the muted group at `0.269`.

## Text has two tiers here

`--foreground` and `--muted-foreground`, and that is the whole content palette. Card and popover
foregrounds resolve to the same value as `--foreground` in both themes, so they are the same tier
under different names.

<Example>
  <p className="text-foreground text-sm">
    <span>text-foreground, the content itself</span>
  </p>
  <p className="text-muted-foreground mt-1 text-sm">
    <span>text-muted-foreground, timestamps, counts, supporting detail</span>
  </p>
</Example>

There is no third tier to reach for, and inventing one with an opacity composite is what `TOK-8`
exists to stop. Quiet text derives from the foreground token at a pinned step, `text-foreground/60`,
`/70` or `/80`, and that set may only shrink; a new step is a rule change carrying a new
measurement.

## The pairing that keeps failing

`text-muted-foreground` on `bg-muted`, `bg-accent` or `bg-secondary` measures 4.34:1 in light mode
against a 4.5:1 minimum, because the foreground resolves to `#737373` and all three grounds resolve
to `#f5f5f5`. One failure with three spellings. The rule is unconditional rather than light-only:
the same three names are one value in dark too, and the ban is what keeps a component from
depending on which theme it is read in.

Three rules cover the three shapes this takes.

- `TOK-4` catches the pairing inside one quoted class string, statically, before a browser runs.
- `TOK-5` catches it split across a `cva()` base and a variant value, which is how
  `components/ui/tabs.tsx` shipped it.
- `TOK-6` is the cross-component shape, muted text in a child under an ancestor's muted background.
  Every instance that actually shipped broken was this shape, no static rule can see it, and the
  record declares itself unchecked and names `pnpm test:stories` as its checker.

The fix that reaches a composed child is a rebind on the element painting the surface,
`[--muted-foreground:var(--accent-foreground)]`, rather than restyling somebody else's slot.
`bg-muted/50` is not a fix: it only clears the bar by blending toward whatever sits behind it, and
the number moves with that background.

## Destructive goes solid, never a tint

`text-destructive` on a translucent `bg-destructive/NN` measures 4.0:1 alone and worse once tints
compound. Use `bg-destructive text-background`, which inverts against the saturated fill in both
themes and clears the bar in both: computed from the shipped values, 4.76:1 in light and 6.84:1 in
dark. This token set defines no `--destructive-foreground`, so that is not an option here. The
solid pairing is what `promo-card.tsx` and `generation-queue.tsx` both landed on.

## No gradients, and violet has nowhere to go

`COL-1` is a blocker: no `bg-gradient-*`, no `bg-clip-text`, no `backdrop-blur` in product UI. Flat
is the position, and a gradient is emphasis standing in for hierarchy. Two files are exempt by
name in the record.

`COL-6` is a warning that permits indigo, violet and purple only as `chart-4`. Worth knowing before
reading that as a licence: measured 2026-09-09, `--chart-4` in this theme is `oklch(0.371 0 0)`, a
neutral grey in both modes. The rule's one sanctioned channel currently carries no hue, so in
practice it reads as a flat ban.

## Marketing palettes live outside the theme

The 15 marketing items are the one place saturated colour ships, and it is scoped away from the
product tokens. `apps/storybook/src/marketing.css` declares 13 `--marketing-*` custom properties in
`:root` and overrides 11 of them in `.dark`: five rainbow steps, four aurora steps, two beam
endpoints, and pulse and ripple colours that point at `--primary`.

A consumer installing a marketing item gets those properties written into their stylesheet by the
registry and can retune them without touching the product theme. Nothing in `super-ai` reads them.

## Rules

- Pair a surface with its own foreground. `text-foreground` on `bg-muted` is about 18:1;
  `text-accent-foreground` on `bg-accent` is about 16:1.
- Never `text-muted-foreground` on `bg-muted`, `bg-accent` or `bg-secondary`, at any size, in any
  state, including inside a component you are composing.
- Reach for a composed child with a variable rebind on the surface, never by restyling its slot.
- Destructive fills go solid. A translucent destructive tint under destructive text fails.
- Quiet text is `text-foreground` at `/60`, `/70` or `/80`. Any other composite is a `TOK-8`
  finding.
- Colour is never the only signal. The a11y gate has caught state conveyed by colour alone before;
  see **Foundations → Inclusive design**.

Live values and the full token inventory are on **Foundations → Design Tokens**. The contrast
audit that produced every number here is `docs/design-system/a11y-baseline.md`.
