# AI conversation

A conversation surface is a stream of turns and a composer, and this repo hand-rolls neither.
`chat-shell.tsx` lines 6 and 7 import AI Elements' `Conversation` and `Message`, and everything
around them is a catalog component with its own props and its own accessibility contract. Three
registry sources reach into `apps/storybook/src/components/ai-elements/` at all (measured
2026-09-10): `chat-shell.tsx` and `notebook-shell.tsx` take `conversation` and `message`,
`suggestion-chips.tsx` takes `suggestion`. This page owns what goes into a turn, what the reader
attaches before sending one, and what happens when the model stops and asks. **Foundations → App
structure** owns the shell those parts sit in, and **Patterns → Actions & confirmation** owns the
gates in depth.

## Use it when

- The product's main verb is asking, and the answer arrives as prose rather than as a rendered
  asset.
- The reader's selection, files and mentions are the prompt, so the composer has to hold references
  as well as text.
- An answer has to be checkable against documents, claim by claim.
- The model can act on the world, so some turns are requests for permission rather than replies.

Do not use it when the answer is one of these instead:

- **The result is an asset.** A generation with a preview, a queue and a retry belongs to
  **Patterns → Loading states**, whatever surface started it.
- **The wait itself.** Where the wait is drawn, and how many indicators one wait may show, is that
  same page.
- **A single confirmation.** Cost before commit, the four approval verbs and the permission dialog
  are all specified at **Patterns → Actions & confirmation**; this page only places them in a
  stream.
- **A standing fact.** A provider that stopped answering or a quota nearly spent rides the thing it
  is about, per **Patterns → Status & feedback**.

`conversation.tsx` line 17 puts `role="log"` on the stream, which is the announcement the whole
surface rests on, and line 14 leaves the root at `overflow-y-hidden`. Scrolling is the caller's
job: `chat-shell.tsx` lines 100 to 110 records that `ConversationContent` inserts its own scroll
element and sets no overflow on it, so a stream without a `scrollClassName` grows rather than
scrolls. The example above passes the same two classes `chat-shell` does for that reason. Because
the class handle lands on an element the caller does not otherwise own, the tab stop and the
accessible name have to sit on `Conversation` itself, which is what `chat-shell.tsx` lines 385 to
390 do with `aria-label` and `tabIndex`.

A turn is `Message` plus `MessageContent`. `message.tsx` lines 17 to 26 set an `is-user` or
`is-assistant` class from the `from` prop, and lines 30 to 41 hang every visual decision off it:
a user turn gets a `bg-secondary` bubble with its own padding and radius, an assistant turn gets
plain `text-foreground` and no container at all. The asymmetry is the point, since the assistant's
turn is the one that carries structure inside it.

What goes inside is the caller's decision. `chat-shell.tsx` lines 225 to 234 record that the shell
deliberately skips AI Elements' markdown renderer and takes a `ReactNode`, so a host that renders
its own markdown is not fighting a renderer it did not choose. Lines 246 to 248 gate the per-turn
rating to assistant turns, since there is nothing to rate about your own message.

### What comes back, claim by claim

The block in the stream above is `answer-block`, and it is built around one decision recorded at
`answer-block.tsx` line 16: citations attach to the claim, not to the answer. Everything else
follows from that.

- Coverage is computed, not passed. `coverageOf` at lines 30 to 36 returns `cited`,
  `partially-cited` or `uncited`, and line 33 is explicit that partial coverage must not read as
  full coverage.
- A gap is stated in words. Lines 88 and 89 ship the two warning strings, one for an answer with no
  sources at all and one for an answer where some claims are unmarked.
- The unused count is shown, not hidden. Lines 96 to 101 render how many retrieved sources went
  into nothing, on the reasoning at lines 93 to 95 that this is how a reader notices the model
  ignored the relevant document.
- Streaming order is claim then citation. Line 60 holds the last claim's markers back while
  `streaming` is set, because a marker that arrives before the text it supports reads as a load
  failure.

The markers themselves are `citation-ref`. Line 8 gives it three states, `resolved`, `loading` and
`unresolved`. Line 59 returns the bare marker for the two that are not resolved, so only a resolved
citation opens a hovercard. A `loading` marker pulses through `animate-pulse motion-reduce:animate-none`
at line 49. An `unresolved` one still renders, still says it is unresolved, and carries its own
`aria-label`, since a citation that quietly disappears is how a document stops being checkable. The
hovercard carries the quoted chunk and not only the document title, on the prop comment at line 15:
a citation that only names a document is not verifiable.

Hover marker 1 in the example above and read the quote. Then read **Foundations → States** for the
vocabulary these state names come from.

`context-chips.tsx` line 32 names four reference kinds, `file`, `selection`, `url` and `mention`,
and `unresolved` sits across all four as a separate axis rather than as a fifth kind. A chip whose
target moved says so four ways at once: the icon swaps to a warning glyph, the border goes dashed,
the label is struck through, and line 73 appends the literal word `unresolved`. The remove control
changes its own name to match at line 77, so the two chips are distinguishable by name and not only
by look. Overflow is a real control with a counted name at line 102 rather than a clipped row.

`quote-reply.tsx` line 25 names four capture sources, `text-range`, `image-region`, `table-cell`
and `timeline-range`, and they are one component rather than four because only the excerpt branches.
The load-bearing prop is `anchor`, documented at lines 44 to 51: the excerpt is what the reader
sees, the anchor is what the quote resolves against after the source is edited. Line 96 renders the
anchor in a `cite` element beside the kind label, so a quote always says where it came from.

`model-picker.tsx` line 50 gives one component three containers, `dropdown`, `expanded-cards` and
`node-inline`, with the same rows in each. Line 58 groups models by task signature and never

Lines 78 to 87 are worth reading before you compose a selected row anywhere. A selected row paints

recurring failure in this system, and `TOK-4`, `TOK-5` and `TOK-6` in
`packages/ds-rules/rules/local.json` are the rules that catch it in a class string.

## The composer assembles the context

The design specification's section 4 states the principle this whole family exists for: the composer
is a context-assembly surface, where selections become quotes, entities become chips and mentions
resolve to references. `chat-shell` implements that literally. Its composer region at lines 431 to
462 is `media-prompt-bar` in its docked presentation, with `context-chips` passed into the
`contextChips` slot, `mode-tabs` into `settings`, and `disclaimer-note` under the whole thing. The
chips prop is documented at line 195 as references attached to the next message, which is what makes
removing one a real edit rather than deleting a word.

Two consequences are worth stating.

**No registry source imports `prompt-input`.** All 23 AI Elements ports are available at
`apps/storybook/src/components/ai-elements/`, and `prompt-input.tsx` is the largest of them, but the
catalog's own composer is `media-prompt-bar` (measured 2026-09-10). Reach for `PromptInput` when you
want the vendored attachment handling and its submit-status button; reach for `media-prompt-bar` when
you want the catalog's locked state, cost line and reference strip. Do not run both in one composer.

**A shell can suppress an affordance it does not need.** `chat-shell.tsx` line 98 hides the
composer's negative-prompt toggle with `display: none`, which takes it out of the tab order as well
as out of sight, and the comment says to delete the class the moment the composer grows a real
opt-out. Copy the note along with the technique.

The one variable below is how attached context is rendered.

## When the model asks

Some turns are requests. Three surfaces cover them, and which one you reach for depends on what is
being decided.

<Example>
  <ApprovalCardDemo />
</Example>

**An artifact needs a decision.** `approval-card` is the Approval contract's only implementation in
the catalog, so it sets the shape the rest of the families inherit. Lines 43 to 48 fix the four
verbs and their order, Confirm then Edit then Regenerate then Skip, in the component rather than at
the call site, so a caller who passes two handlers still gets them in the house order. Confirm and
Skip end the interaction; Edit and Regenerate hand the reader back to the artifact. Line 92 keeps
Undo on screen for eight seconds after a resolution, since the two terminal verbs would otherwise be
irreversible. Detail stays behind an explicit expand and is absent from the DOM until then. This card
belongs in the stream, as the turn that is waiting on you.

**A side effect needs permission.** `permission-prompt` is an `AlertDialog` and renders through a
portal with a backdrop, so it cannot be shown honestly inside a docs column. It is live at its own
size at [Super AI → Permission Prompt](?path=/docs/super-ai-permission-prompt--docs). Its four verbs
are defaulted at lines 118 to 121, Allow once, Always allow, Deny and Edit first. It interrupts
rather than joining the stream, on the reasoning that a consequential call has already been composed
and is paused. **Patterns → Actions & confirmation** carries the three rules its tests pin.

**A standing grant needs reviewing.** `permission-prompt` emits the Always allow choice and stops
there, and `autonomy-selector` is where the resulting grant becomes visible and revocable. Line 12
gives three levels, `ask`, `auto-reads` and `full`, and lines 43 to 50 pair each with a sentence
naming its blast radius rather than a label alone. Denials render as their own list beside the
grants, because deny wins outright and takes no exceptions. A product that can create a permanent
permission and cannot show the ones already granted is a one-way door, which is why these two ship
together.

## Key decisions

- **Compose the stream, do not rebuild it.** `Conversation` and `Message` come from AI Elements
  through a cross-registry dependency, and three registry sources use them rather than forking a
  bubble.
- **Pass `scrollClassName`, or the stream does not scroll.** The element that is supposed to scroll
  is created by the vendored component and left at `overflow: visible`.
- **Name the stream and give it a tab stop on `Conversation` itself.** The inner scroll element is
  not addressable for anything else.
- **Citations attach to claims.** An answer-level source list cannot say which sentence came from
  where, and coverage cannot be computed from it.
- **Show the gap.** Unsourced claims and unused retrieved sources are both rendered, because a thin
  answer that looks complete is the failure this family exists to prevent.
- **A broken reference stays visible.** Both `citation-ref` and `context-chips` render an unresolved
  target in a state that says so, rather than dropping it.
- **The anchor outlives the excerpt.** A quote resolves against a stable position, so an edit to the
  source does not silently re-point it.
- **Chips fill the composer; they never send.** Suggestion chips are prompts, and overflow is a real
  link.
- **Group models by what they do.** Task signature first, with runtime and hardware in badge text.
- **The four approval verbs are the component's property.** Order is fixed once, so the muscle memory
  holds across every approval surface.
- **Grant and revoke ship together.** Always allow only emits a choice; the review surface is where
  it becomes safe.

## Rules

- Compose `Conversation`, `ConversationContent`, `Message` and `MessageContent` from
  `@/components/ai-elements/*` rather than writing a stream. Pass `scrollClassName`, and put
  `aria-label` and `tabIndex` on `Conversation`.
- Let `from` drive the turn's appearance. Never restyle a user turn into an assistant turn or paint
  a bubble by hand.
- Attach every citation to the claim it supports, and let the coverage state be computed from the
  claims rather than passed in.
- Render an unresolved citation or an unresolved chip. Never drop one, and never signal it by colour
  alone: `context-chips` uses an icon swap, a dashed border, a strikethrough and the word together.
- Hold a streaming claim's markers back until the claim itself has settled.
- Keep the quote's anchor independent of its excerpt, and render the anchor.
- Wire a suggestion chip's `onSelect` to fill the composer. Never to submit and never to navigate.
- Give an overflow control a real destination and a counted accessible name.
- Build a picker trigger's accessible name from the label and the current value together, and put
  runtime, hardware and price in badge text.
- Put the approval card in the stream and the permission dialog over it. A permission gate that
  scrolls away is not a gate.
- Never let a surface write a standing grant without a surface that lists and revokes them.
- Never write `text-muted-foreground` on `bg-muted`, `bg-accent` or `bg-secondary`. A selected row
  and a user turn are both composed grounds, and `model-picker.tsx` lines 78 to 87 is what the fix
  costs after the fact. `TOK-4`, `TOK-5` and `TOK-6` are the rules.
- Check a vendored AI Elements port before you extend it. `check:tokens` scopes `TOK-3` to
  `apps/docs/registry/super-ai`, `apps/docs/registry/marketing` and `apps/docs/components/ui`
  (`packages/ds-rules/rules/local.json`), so the palette classes in `tool.tsx` lines 45 to 50 are
  outside the gate rather than approved by it.

## Content notes

Write the assistant's turn as an answer, then let the markers carry the evidence. The two coverage
warnings are already written and shipped, and they are the register to match: plain, specific about
what is missing, and no apology.

Reference labels are the thing itself. `brand-guide.pdf`, `lines 12-40`, `@teammate`, `Q3 Budget!C12`,
`0:42-0:51`. A chip that reads "attached file" has thrown away the only information it had.

Suggestion chips are verbs. "Summarize this document", "Draft a reply", "Continue from last
template". Sentence case, no trailing punctuation, and one action each. `CPY-1` is the rule that a
generic label is not a label.

The four approval verbs and the four permission verbs are fixed strings, not synonyms to reword per
product. Confirm, Edit, Regenerate, Skip. Allow once, Always allow, Deny, Edit first.

The disclaimer under a composer defaults to "AI can make mistakes. Check important info."
(`disclaimer-note.tsx` line 15). Override it with something true about your product rather than
deleting it.

**Content → Voice & principles** owns the register, **Content → Error messages** owns what a failed
turn says, **Foundations → Inclusive design** owns the gate every one of these examples has to pass,
and **Foundations → App structure** owns the shell this whole page sits inside.
