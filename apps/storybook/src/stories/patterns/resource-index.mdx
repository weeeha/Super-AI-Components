# Resource index

The screen that says what exists. An index lets a reader reach the record they came for, and this
registry answers it with five views behind one switcher rather than three separate components:
`data-views` renders list, kanban, table, calendar and timeline from one `items` array, and
`use-view-mode` holds which one is showing. The choice between them is the reader's, stored per
section, which is why the pattern is mostly about what the app decides for them: which views are
offered at all, how the collection is narrowed, and what groups it.

## Use it when

- A region lists many records of one kind, each with a name and a place to go.
- The reader came to find one of them, or to see how many there are.
- The collection outgrows the screen, so it needs narrowing, grouping, or both.

Do not use it when the answer is one of these instead:

- **One record.** That is **Patterns → Resource details**.
- **A list you choose from to fill a field.** `select`, `combobox` and `command` exist to be chosen
  from and close when you pick. **Patterns → Forms & wizards** owns them.
- **A route rather than a record.** `app-sidebar`, `sidebar-nav` and `breadcrumb` move a reader
  through the app; **Foundations → Navigation** owns them.
- **A summary of the collection.** `stat-readout` and `quota-meter` report a number.
  **Patterns → Status & feedback** owns them.
- **A conversation.** `thread-list` looks like an index and is one, but the panes it opens are
  **Patterns → AI conversation**'s.
- **Nothing to list.** `empty-state` and the Empty contract in `docs/design-system/concept-model.md`
  section 4 cover that, and **Content → Empty states** owns the words.

## The index, live

Four units make up the pattern here: the switcher over the collection, the narrowing controls, the
grouper, and the records themselves.

Five modes, one array. `use-view-mode.tsx` line 24 declares `ViewMode` as `list`, `kanban`, `table`,
`calendar` and `timeline`, and line 27 fixes that as both the switcher's order and its arrow-key
order, with the two time views last. Line 30 marks `calendar` and `timeline` as the views that need a
date per item, and `availableViewModes` at line 43 drops them when a section has no date accessor,
because offering a view that can only render empty is worse than not offering it. The default at line
32 is `kanban`.

Each view earns its own keyboard and screen-reader treatment, and the reasons are recorded at the
sites. `table-view.tsx` line 28 writes `role="grid"` rather than shipping a plain table, so a row can
be a legal focus target and carry `aria-selected`; line 21 derives `interactive` from whether
`onItemClick` was passed, so a read-only table advertises no tab stop that does nothing.
`kanban-view.tsx` line 20 makes the board a `section` with `tabIndex` 0 and the name "Board columns",
because a horizontally scrolling container that cannot take focus leaves a keyboard user stranded at
the second column. `feed-view.tsx` line 33 paints its sticky group header `text-foreground` on
`bg-muted/60` rather than `text-muted-foreground`, which is the 4.34:1 pairing
`docs/design-system/a11y-baseline.md` records and `TOK-4` bans.

### Narrowing: chips, then a rail

Two components, one ladder. `filter-bar.tsx` is 98 lines and exports four symbols: `FilterBar`,
`FilterChip`, `AddFilterChip` and `FiltersButton`. `filter-panel.tsx` is 507 lines, and its own header
comment states the escalation: the bar handles a handful of chips, and past roughly six facets a
surface moves to the rail. The rail's rules are enforced rather than left to the call site, `count` is
a required field on every facet and part of the checkbox's accessible name, and a facet whose count is
zero renders disabled, still shows its zero, and carries an sr-only "no matches". The dead end is
stated before the click and stated in text, never by dimming alone.

Both components pay the muted-on-muted tax explicitly. `filter-bar.tsx` line 32 rebinds
`--muted-foreground` to `var(--secondary-foreground)` on an active chip, because the chip paints
`bg-secondary` and the remove button inside it carries its own `text-muted-foreground` that a
slot-level override cannot reach. That rebind-on-the-painting-element move is the house fix for
`TOK-6`, and the same line appears in `cost-chip.tsx` line 25 and `gen-settings-bar.tsx` line 33.

`record-list.tsx` is the index shape for records that **run**, and its header comment carries three
rules worth repeating on a page like this. The enable toggle is the primary control and sits in the

## Who chooses the view

The switcher is the reader's control, and that is the decision this repo has already made rather than
one each screen re-takes. `use-view-mode.tsx` line 48 keys the preference as
`super-ai.view-mode.<section>` in `localStorage`, so a reader's choice survives a reload and is scoped
per section rather than per app. The app's remaining job is to decide which views a section may offer,
by supplying a date accessor or not.

The hook's docblock carries a constraint worth reading before wiring it. A given storage key must be
owned by exactly one hook instance, because `storage` events do not fire in the tab that wrote the
value, so two instances sharing a key drift silently. That is the stated reason `data-views` and
`detail-view-shell` both take their mode as a prop instead of calling the hook themselves.

The record axis is separate and never consults the collection axis. `DetailMode` at line 62 is
`popup`, `overlay` or `fullscreen`, keyed per entity, so a section can be a table whose rows open in a
popup while another is a board opening into an overlay. **Patterns → Resource details** owns what
happens inside each of the three.

## Key decisions

- **One collection, one items array.** `data-views` takes `items` once and switches presentation over
  it; `asset-library.tsx` makes the same call for folders and files, which share one table and one set
  of columns because a separate folder pane forces a reader to think about storage. A second array for
  a second view is how two views start disagreeing.
- **Offer only the views that can render.** `availableViewModes` is the mechanism, and the rule behind
  it is that an empty calendar teaches a reader the section is broken.
- **The narrowing control is picked by facet count.** Chips to about six, then the rail. Neither is a
  new component, and inventing a third is not the fix.
- **Every facet states its count, including zero.** A filter that will return nothing says so first,
  and says it in text and in a programmatic disabled state.
- **A row is a target, not a toolbar.** `asset-library.tsx` exposes exactly one action control per
  row, an overflow menu trigger revealed on hover and on focus-within. **Patterns → Actions and
  confirmation** decides what goes in it.
- **Group by date unless the data has a better spine.** It is the one grouper that works on threads,
  generations and inboxes alike.
- **The view preference belongs to the reader, per section.** Do not reset it on navigation, and do
  not let two components own one storage key.

## Rules

- An index lists records that already exist. A list you pick from to fill a field is a picker and
  closes when you choose; a list that moves you through the app is navigation. Neither is this pattern.
- One `items` array per collection. `data-views` switches presentation over it and never takes a
  second source per view.
- Only offer the time views when the section supplies a date per item. `availableViewModes` in
  `use-view-mode.tsx` line 43 is the check, and it exists so no reader meets an empty calendar.
- Keep the view order `list`, `kanban`, `table`, `calendar`, `timeline`, per `VIEW_MODE_VALUES` in
  `use-view-mode.tsx` line 27. It is the arrow-key order as well as the visual one.
- One owner per preference key. `usePersistedPreference` does not receive `storage` events from its
  own tab, so a second instance on the same key drifts. Thread the value down as a prop, the way
  `data-views` and `detail-view-shell` do.
- A table whose rows do nothing takes no tab stop. `table-view.tsx` line 21 derives that from
  `onItemClick`, so passing a handler is what turns rows into focus targets, `role="grid"` cells and
  `aria-selected` state.
- A scroll container takes focus and a name. `kanban-view.tsx` line 20 is a `section` with `tabIndex`
  0 and `aria-label="Board columns"`, because a bare `div` is `role="generic"` and ARIA prohibits a
  name on it.
- Never write `text-muted-foreground` on `bg-muted`, `bg-accent` or `bg-secondary`, in one class
  string or split across elements. `TOK-4`, `TOK-5` and `TOK-6` are the rules, `check:tokens` enforces
  them over registry sources, and the cross-element case is the one a lint cannot see, so a component
  that paints its own muted surface rebinds `--muted-foreground` on that surface. `filter-bar.tsx`
  line 32 is the shipped precedent.
- Filter chips to about six facets, then the rail. `filter-panel.tsx` states the threshold in its own
  header comment.
- Every facet carries a count, and the count is part of the checkbox's accessible name. A zero-count
  facet renders disabled, keeps its zero visible, and carries an sr-only "no matches".
- A record's enable toggle stays in the row. `record-list.tsx` puts it there deliberately, one
  keystroke from the list rather than behind a menu.
- Icon clusters are `aria-hidden` and the names beside them carry the meaning. A cluster that only
  exists as marks tells a screen reader nothing.
- Row actions live in one overflow menu per row. **Patterns → Actions & confirmation** owns what is
  in it and which entries are destructive.
- The empty collection empties the records region and leaves the narrowing controls standing, so the
  filter that emptied it stays reachable. Every panel, list, grid and pane ships an empty state, per
  the Empty contract in `docs/design-system/concept-model.md` section 4.
- A record's own view is **Patterns → Resource details**. The index hands it off rather than growing
  into it.

## Content notes

A collection names itself once, at the top, and counts itself as a numeral however small. The count is
the collection's until a filter applies, then the filtered count, which is the number
**Content → Empty states** needs when the region comes back empty.

Group labels are the shortest true thing: "Today", "Yesterday", "In review". `date-section-demo`
ships the first two and `data-views-demo` groups by bucket, and neither adds a word the reader can see
from the rows themselves.

Filter chip labels are the facet's own name, singular, sentence case, no count in the label:
`filter-bar-demo` ships "Genre", "Instrument", "Mood" and an add-chip reading "Owner". The count lives
beside the facet in the rail, where `filter-panel.tsx` requires it, rather than in the chip.

A row's subtitle carries state as words. `record-list-demo` writes "Last run 4 min ago", "Running
since 12:04" and "Last run failed, auth expired" rather than colouring the row, because colour with no
label names nothing. **Patterns → Status & feedback** owns which surface reports a record's state,
and `CPY-1` requires the label to say the thing.

This page owns how a collection is presented, narrowed and grouped. **Content → Empty states** owns
what the region says when it holds nothing, **Patterns → Actions & confirmation** owns what a row's
menu can do, and **Patterns → Resource details** owns what happens once the reader picks one.
