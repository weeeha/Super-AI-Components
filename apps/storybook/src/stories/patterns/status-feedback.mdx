# Status & feedback

Status is what the interface says about something the reader did not just do: a provider that
stopped answering, a quota nearly spent, a limit that will clear in two minutes, a job still
running in another tab. One question sorts every surface below. **Is this a standing fact about a
thing, or an event that has already finished?** A fact rides the thing it is about and stays true
until it is not. An event passes. This registry answers that question one way almost everywhere,
and the evidence is a grep: no source under `apps/docs/registry/super-ai/` imports sonner or calls
`toast()` (measured 2026-09-09). Every status this repo ships is drawn in place.

## Use it when

- A dependency the product runs on changed state, and the reader can do something about it.
- A budget is being spent and the useful moment is before it runs out.
- A constraint is holding the reader up and time will clear it on its own.
- Work the reader started is running somewhere they are not looking.
- A result is finished and the product wants the reader's judgment on it.

Do not use it when the answer is one of these instead:

- **A wait with a result coming.** **Patterns → Loading states** owns where the wait is drawn and
  which instrument reports it.
- **A region with nothing in it.** **Content → Empty states** owns all three species, including
  the one where the request failed.
- **A failure.** **Content → Error messages** already maps every failure to its surface, and that
  mapping wins over the question above.
- **The price of a control, or a gate in front of it.** **Patterns → Actions & confirmation** owns
  cost, approval and permission.
- **A row reporting its own state inside a list.** **Patterns → Resource index** owns the row.

Both components split their range at the same place and for the same reason. `quota-meter.tsx`

clamps the bar while leaving the numbers honest, so an over-limit row still reports the real

### A constraint that time will clear

<Example>
  <RateLimitBannerDemo />
</Example>

`rate-limit-banner.tsx` line 44 has two causes, `your-limit` and `provider-capacity`, and the
`COPY` table at line 51 is fixed per cause and deliberately not overridable by prop. The header
comment at lines 46 to 50 says why: if the caller could pass the title, the distinction would
erode back into one generic string. Your plan's cap and the provider's weather are different
facts, and each body line names which thing is not wrong.

Three more properties follow from the surface persisting. The countdown is a prop, not a timer:
lines 24 to 28 record that the host owns the clock, so the banner cannot desync from the reset the
host actually knows about, and can be server-rendered. The root is `role="note"` at line 179
rather than the `role="alert"` the vendored `Alert` defaults to, because an assertive region
wrapping a value that changes every second would interrupt a screen reader once a second forever.
And the announcement is throttled by content rather than by a timer: `countdownAnnouncement` at
lines 116 to 123 buckets the remaining time into minutes or hours, so the `sr-only` polite region
at line 258 speaks at most once a minute while the visible clock at lines 209 to 215 ticks on.

Where there is no estimate, line 222 says so and offers the opt-in, rather than writing "try again
later" and asking the reader to poll.

### Work that is happening elsewhere

<Example>
  <TaskTrayDemo />
</Example>

`task-tray.tsx` line 18 names four statuses, `running`, `needs-input`, `done` and `failed`, and
line 59 sorts them in an order that is an argument rather than an alphabet: `needs-input` first,
`failed` second, `running` and `done` last. The comment at lines 56 to 58 makes the case. A
background task blocked on an approval is invisible work that has silently stopped, and it is the
only row the reader must act on; a failure is over but is still news; running and done are
reporting. Line 34 offers cancel for anything still live, because work you cannot stop burns
budget and trust.

This is the same fact-versus-event split at list scale. The tray holds facts about jobs, so it
stays available behind its trigger; nothing about it expires.

### The note that never leaves

  <DisclaimerNoteDemo />

`disclaimer-note.tsx` is the limit case of a standing fact: it has no `dismissed` state and no

## A fact or an event

Two sub-questions decide the surface, and the second one is where this registry differs from most.

**Is it about a thing or about a moment?** A thing's state travels with it and renders wherever
the thing renders: a provider row, a quota row, a credit pill, a tray row. A moment has no thing
to ride, so it needs a surface of its own.

**Does it persist or pass?** Every status surface in this registry persists. The one primitive
that passes is `sonner`, vendored at `apps/storybook/src/components/ui/sonner.tsx` and live at
[shadcn/ui → Sonner](?path=/docs/shadcn-ui-sonner--docs). Nothing in the registry mounts it, which
is a deliberate reading of the design spec's principle 6 in section 4: failure is a first-class
state rendered inline where the result would have been, never only a toast. **Content → Error
messages** states the same finding from the failure side.

That leaves a narrow, real job for a toast in a consuming app: an event that finished somewhere
the reader was not looking, that needs no action, and that nothing on the page can carry. Before
reaching for it, check the thing the news is about. If the thing is on screen, the news belongs on
the thing.

## How loudly it announces

Announcement is part of the choice, not a styling afterthought, and this registry spreads its
surfaces across the ladder on purpose.

- `role="alert"` interrupts. The vendored `Alert` sets it by default, which is why
  `rate-limit-banner.tsx` line 179 overrides it back to `note` rather than inheriting it.
- `role="status"` waits its turn. `env-status.tsx` line 184 and `feedback.tsx` line 257 both take
  it, for a state that changes after mount.
- `role="note"` announces nothing on arrival and is read when the reader reaches it.
- A badge, a bar or a pill carries no role at all. `quota-meter.tsx` line 92 is the exception that
  proves the shape: its track is `role="progressbar"` named by the row's own visible label through
  `aria-labelledby` at line 93, added because every track was otherwise announcing as an unnamed
  progressbar.

Pick the surface whose interruption matches the news, then let the words carry the rest.

The one variable below is whether the condition is stated.

## Key decisions

- **A standing fact rides the thing it is about.** If it is still true after any announcement
  would have gone, it belongs on the row, the pill or the meter.
- **Two failures with different remedies are two states.** `env-status` splits three failure
  states apart rather than collapsing them, because the remedy is the part the reader needs.
- **The alarm colours the surface, not the text.** `credits-indicator.tsx` lines 29 to 34 and
  `env-status.tsx` lines 138 to 150 both go solid rather than tinting a foreground, on measured
  contrast.
- **The useful state is the one before the limit.** Both budget components carry a middle state
  for that reason, and `quota-meter` keeps one row per resource so the aggregate cannot hide it.
- **The host owns the clock.** `rate-limit-banner` and `quota-meter` both take a preformatted or
  per-tick value rather than running a timer, so the surface cannot drift from the real reset.
- **A live region's content is what gets throttled, not its timer.** A coarse phrase that changes
  once a minute is the fix for a ticking countdown, per `rate-limit-banner.tsx` lines 110 to 115.
- **Fixed copy is a feature where a distinction matters.** `rate-limit-banner`'s two titles are
  not props, so a caller cannot flatten the two causes into one string.
- **Anything the reader must act on stays put.** The tray sorts `needs-input` first for the same
  reason nothing here is a toast.

## Rules

- Sort every message by two questions: thing or moment, persists or passes. A thing's state
  renders on the thing.
- Never carry a state by colour alone. Every state gets a word in visible text, outside
  `aria-hidden`, as `env-status.tsx` lines 27 to 31 require.
- Give every failure state its own remedy sentence. A state with no stated next step is a dead
  end, which is the argument for four `env-status` states rather than two.
- Split a range at the moment before it runs out, not at zero. `near-limit` and `low` are their
  own states, not shades of normal.
- Report one resource per row. An aggregate hides the one the reader is about to hit.
- Clamp the bar, never the number. An over-limit row still reports the real overage.
- Put an alarm on the surface, never on the foreground. `text-destructive` on `bg-muted` measures
  4.37:1 against a 4.5:1 minimum, and `text-warning` is lighter still.
- Never write `text-muted-foreground` on `bg-muted`, `bg-accent` or `bg-secondary`. `TOK-4`,
  `TOK-5` and `TOK-6` are the rules and `check:tokens` reports them.
- Accept the countdown as a prop and hold no timer. The host knows the real reset; a self-ticking
  component desyncs from it and cannot be server-rendered.
- Keep a per-second value out of the live region. Announce a bucketed phrase instead, and leave
  the visible clock outside any live region.
- Match the role to the news: `role="alert"` for something that must interrupt, `role="status"`
  for a state that changed after mount, `role="note"` for a standing condition, no role for a
  badge. Override the vendored default when the surface persists.
- Name every progress track from its own visible label with `aria-labelledby`, rather than
  duplicating the text into an `aria-label` that can drift.
- Keep a retraction on anything the reader submitted. `feedback` renders Undo in `submitted`
  unconditionally.
- Do not add a dismiss control to a standing disclaimer. If the message is worth showing it stays
  for the lifetime of the output it qualifies.
- Do not reach for a toast because a surface is missing. Nothing in this registry uses one, and a
  fact spent on a moment leaves the thing it was about saying nothing.
- When the news is a failure, **Content → Error messages** picks the surface. This page does not
  re-decide it.

## Content notes

Tense follows the surface, because the surface already made a claim about time. A standing state
reads in the present: "Degraded. Requests are succeeding, but answering slower than usual."
A remedy reads as an instruction with a verb the reader can act on: "Replace the key for this
provider in Connections." A limit that will clear names the wait rather than the reader's
patience: "Resets in", "Estimated wait", and where there is no estimate, "No reset time reported
yet."

Nothing on this page blames the reader for a constraint. `rate-limit-banner.tsx`'s two body lines
exist to say which thing is not wrong, at lines 67 and 77, because a busy model reading as "you
did something" is the failure the component was written to prevent.

Say the number when there is one. `quota-meter` renders used against limit with the unit, and
`credits-indicator` renders the balance with its unit, both `tabular-nums` so a changing value
does not jitter. **Content → Voice & principles** owns that rule and **Content → Writing
mechanics** owns the marks.

This page owns which surface carries a fact or an event. **Patterns → Loading states** owns the
wait before the fact exists, **Content → Error messages** owns what a failure says, **Content →
Empty states** owns a region with nothing in it, and **Foundations → States** owns the state
vocabulary every one of these surfaces draws from.
