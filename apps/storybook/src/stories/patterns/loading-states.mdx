# Loading states

A wait is drawn in the box the result will occupy, at the size the result will take, and it is
over the moment something replaces it. The vocabulary comes from the state contract in
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-06-10-super-ai-components-design.md` section 6, exported as
`GenerationState` from `apps/docs/registry/super-ai/cost.tsx` line 159: `queued` and `streaming`
are the two waiting names, and `done`, `failed` and `locked` are the three ways out. This page
decides where the wait goes on a screen, how many indicators a screen may show at once, and what
lands in the frame when the wait ends. **Foundations → Motion** decides which instrument reports
it, and **Foundations → States** owns the vocabulary.

## Use it when

- A region is fetching what it will show: a card, a list, a table, a chart.
- A generation has been started and its result has not arrived.
- A batch of jobs is working through a queue and each row is at a different point.
- A control has been pressed and its consequence has not come back.

Do not use it when the answer is one of these instead:

- **The wait is over and nothing came.** That is a zero state, and **Content → Empty states** owns
  all three species, including the one where the request failed.
- **The wait is over and it failed.** **Content → Error messages** owns what the failure says and
  which surface carries it.
- **A standing fact about a thing.** **Patterns → Status & feedback** sorts the surfaces that
  report a state that holds rather than a state that resolves.
- **A page-level condition.** A session that expired or a redirect ends the page rather than
  pausing a region, and no loading instrument covers it.

`result-card.tsx` lines 20 to 23 state the property this demo exists to show: the box is identical
in all six states. Step the card through the cycle and nothing around it moves. Everything that
varies is inside `preview-tile`'s fixed-aspect frame, absolutely positioned over it, or in a
footer whose height is reserved unconditionally, so a grid of these never reflows as results land.

The mapping underneath is worth reading before you add a state anywhere else. Lines 47 to 54 send
the card's six names onto `preview-tile`'s four, with both `queued` and `streaming` collapsing
into the tile's single `loading`. The comment above the map gives the reason: four of the tile's
consumers can never enter `streaming`, so a narrower component should not grow a state it cannot
paint differently. Map at the boundary, and name only what you can draw.

Two of the six are not waits at all. `failed` puts the retry inside the frame, which the prop
comment at line 83 explains as an association problem: a toast loses which result it was about.
Lines 110 to 115 explain why the control moves inside rather than sitting on the frame, since a
frame that is itself a button would nest one interactive element in another and fail axe outright.

per row, and the header comment at lines 20 to 29 gives three rules that all come from the same

a tooltip. Preview and export are separate stages with different economics, so the stage is

drops the settings makes the reader rebuild the job to try again.

`generation-queue.tsx` is the same shape for generations rather than exports, live at

progress are different numbers, and both are needed. Line 115 is the small move worth copying, a
`queued` row whose icon is an `aria-hidden` `Skeleton` rather than a static glyph, because a
queued slot has nothing to measure yet and a reserved shape says so.

Two facts about these four change how you reach for them.

**The primitives are barely used directly.** `apps/storybook/src/components/ui/skeleton.tsx` is
imported by exactly one registry source, `generation-queue.tsx` line 9 (measured 2026-09-09), and
no registry source imports `ui/spinner.tsx` at all. Registry components spin a bare lucide
`Loader2` beside their own visible word instead, which **Foundations → Motion** counts across 13
files. The primitives are still the reference for what a wait announces, which is why they are
drawn here.

**Shimmer is documented and unused.** The design spec's section 6 describes the state contract's
shared conventions as "shimmer while streaming", and `Shimmer` exists here only as the vendored
port at `apps/storybook/src/components/ai-elements/shimmer.tsx`, imported by nothing in the
registry. It also carries no reduced-motion branch: it animates through `motion/react` with
`repeat: Number.POSITIVE_INFINITY`, where the CSS-driven loops around it pair every `animate-*`
with `motion-reduce:animate-none`. Treat the spec's wording as historical, and reach for the
placeholder and glyph loops the registry actually uses.

## Which level is waiting

Decide the level first, then let **Foundations → Motion** pick the instrument. The levels differ
by what the reader can still do while the wait runs.

- **A region.** One card, list, table or chart is fetching inside a page that is otherwise live.
  The region draws placeholders at its own final size, on a container marked `aria-busy="true"`.
- **An item in a list.** Each row is at its own point, so each row carries its own state and its
  own retry. Both queues do this, one state per row rather than one for the list.
- **A control.** The reader pressed something and the consequence has not returned. The control
  keeps its label and reports busy on itself; `run-button.tsx` line 112 sets `aria-busy` on the
  wrapper while `estimating` or `running`, and **Foundations → States** covers the live region
  that goes with it.
- **A stream.** A reply is arriving token by token. The transcript announces the arrival, so the
  indicator beside it stays quiet. **Patterns → AI conversation** owns that surface.

`aria-busy` appears in six registry sources, measured 2026-09-09: `ai-doc-block.tsx`,
`explore-gallery.tsx`, `hero-omnibox.tsx`, `media-prompt-bar.tsx`, `run-button.tsx` and
`selection-toolbar.tsx`. Mark the subtree that is settling, and leave the point announcement to
whichever instrument owns one.

The one variable below is how many indicators report a single fetch.

## When the wait ends

Three exits, and all three land in the frame the wait held.

- **Data.** The result replaces the placeholder in place. Nothing fades in and nothing slides: the
  `animate-pulse` stops because the element is gone, and the content appears where its shape
  already was. `result-card`'s `done` state is the reference.
- **Nothing.** The region is empty for a real reason and says which. `empty-state.tsx` is the
  component and **Content → Empty states** owns the words for "nothing yet" and "nothing matched".
- **Failure.** The request failed, and the failure renders where the result would have been, with
  the way out inside the same frame. `result-card` at `failed`, a queue row at `failed`, a
  `run-button` at `failed`. **Content → Error messages** owns what each of them says.

Nothing in this registry reports the end of a wait in a toast. That is design principle 6 of the
specification's section 4, and **Content → Error messages** records the grep behind it.

## Key decisions

- **The frame is fixed before the fetch.** The region's minimum height is the height the result
  will take, whichever instrument it shows. `result-card`'s reserved footer and
  `preview-tile`'s fixed aspect ratio are how that is achieved rather than asserted.
- **Level first, instrument second.** Region, row, control or stream, then whatever
  **Foundations → Motion** says reports that level.
- **One indicator per wait.** A page placeholder and a region placeholder never stack, a spinner
  never sits inside a skeleton, and a control that is already reporting does not also get a
  banner.
- **`aria-busy` on the subtree, a role on the point.** A skeleton announces nothing and should
  not; a `Spinner` announces itself; a `Progress` root carries its own valuetext.
- **A row owns its own state and its own retry.** Both queues resolve per item, because one failed
  export should not restart three healthy ones.
- **Batch progress and per-item progress are different numbers.** Show both where both exist, per
  `generation-queue.tsx` lines 24 to 26.
- **A queued item shows a reserved shape, not a static glyph.** There is nothing to measure yet,
  and a placeholder says exactly that.
- **The retry lives inside the frame.** A failure reported anywhere else loses which result it was
  about.

## Rules

- Draw the wait where the result lands, at the result's size, and set the region's minimum height
  before the fetch.
- Pick the level, then the instrument. Placeholders for a region or a row, a spinning glyph beside
  a word for a control, `Progress` only where a real proportion exists.
- Show one indicator per wait. Never a nested placeholder and spinner, and never a control's own
  busy state plus a second report of the same action.
- Mark the settling subtree `aria-busy="true"`. Leave `Spinner`'s `role="status"` as the point
  announcement and add no live region of your own inside it.
- Never put a number beside an indeterminate indicator. `Progress` reports a `value`; a spinner
  does not get a percentage.
- Pair every `animate-*` with `motion-reduce:animate-none`, and give the row a visible word so the
  state survives when the motion stops. `render-queue.tsx` lines 72 to 74 is the shipped example,
  with "Rendering" beside the glyph for that reason.
- Keep a component's state union to what it can paint differently, and map at the boundary the way
  `result-card.tsx` lines 47 to 54 maps six names onto four.
- Resolve per item wherever items fail independently, and keep the failed row's own settings so a
  retry re-runs the same job.
- Render the failure inline with the retry in the same frame. A toast loses the association, and
  no registry component uses one.
- Never write `text-muted-foreground` on `bg-muted`, `bg-accent` or `bg-secondary`. A placeholder
  is `bg-muted`, so any label you put beside it is measured against that ground. `TOK-4`, `TOK-5`
  and `TOK-6` are the rules.
- Add no artificial delay before an indicator appears. Nothing here ships a threshold, and a
  reserved frame costs nothing when the fetch is fast.

## Content notes

Name what is being waited on. "Encoding main cut", "Generating 4 images", "Rendering slides". A
bare "Loading" adds nothing the instrument did not already say, and it is the only thing a screen
reader gets from `Spinner`'s own label.

State labels are sentence case and single words where the state is a single fact:
`render-queue.tsx` lines 62 to 67 ships "Queued", "Rendering", "Done" and "Failed", and
`result-card.tsx` lines 57 to 62 ships "Not started", "Queued", "Generating" and "Result ready".
Reach for one of those before inventing a synonym.

A failed wait is written the way **Content → Error messages** writes any failure: what happened,
then the verb the reader can act on. "Encoder ran out of memory" beside a Retry, not "Something
went wrong".

This page owns where a wait is drawn and how it ends. **Foundations → Motion** owns which
instrument moves and how it behaves under reduced motion, **Foundations → States** owns the state
vocabulary, **Content → Empty states** owns the frame with nothing in it, and **Patterns → Status
& feedback** owns the surfaces that report a fact rather than a wait.
