# Forms & wizards

The screens where a reader puts something in. Most forms in an AI app are not sign-up forms, they are
**parameter** forms: the values decide what a model does next, and often what it costs. That changes
the shape of the pattern. This registry ships the row primitive (`field-row`), the panel that stacks
rows into a parameter surface (`parameter-panel`), the compact strip that puts the same settings
beside a prompt (`gen-settings-bar`), the settings surface (`settings-dialog`), and two wizards whose
step machinery is deliberately shared.

## Use it when

- The reader arrived to enter, choose, or change values, and nothing commits until they say so.
- The values configure a run, so the reader needs to understand what each one does to the result.
- Editing a record outgrew its pane, which is the line **Patterns → Resource details** hands over at.
- The work splits into decisions taken one at a time, in an order that matters.

Do not use it when the answer is one of these instead:

- **A control that acts on release.** A filter, a sort, a view mode. Nothing is pending, so nothing is
  submitted. **Patterns → Resource index** owns the bar they sit on.
- **A prompt.** `media-prompt-bar` and the AI Elements `PromptInput` port are composers with a send
  button. **Patterns → AI conversation** owns them.
- **A question with two answers.** `trust-dialog` and `permission-prompt` stop the page to ask, and
  **Patterns → Actions & confirmation** owns what they ask.
- **The commit itself, when it spends money.** `run-button` carries the cost line and the commit, and
  **Patterns → Actions & confirmation** owns the moment.
- **What a failure says.** **Content → Error messages** owns the words of every error here. This page
  owns where in a form they render.

## The form, live

### The row

<Example>
  <FieldRowDemo />
</Example>

`field-row.tsx` is 79 lines and one grid: line 23 is `grid-cols-[6rem_1fr]`, a 96px label column and a
control that takes the rest. Three things about it decide how everything above it behaves.

The label is a real `label` with `htmlFor` pointing at an id from `React.useId()`, and the control is
a **render prop**: `children(controlId, describedBy)`. A caller cannot forget to wire the id, because
the id is what the function receives. When `hint` is passed the row mints a second id and hands it
back as `describedBy`, so the hint reaches the control through `aria-describedby` rather than sitting
near it visually. `field-row-demo` shows both forms, one row with a hint and one without.

`UnitInput` is the compact numeric control this family keeps reaching for, and it is written logically
rather than physically: line 61 uses `text-end` rather than `text-right`, and line 72 uses `pe-2`
rather than `pr-2`, both with the comment that under `dir="rtl"` the unit suffix moves to the visual
left and the value has to stay against it. The design spec section 4, under Selection and objects,
records the requirement it satisfies: editor fields carry units, percent, seconds and multiplier
suffixes in compact inputs.

### The parameter panel

<Example>
  <ParameterPanelDemo />
</Example>

`parameter-panel.tsx` line 19 states the relationship: every row composes `FieldRow` for its label,
control, hint and reset grid rather than restyling it. That is what keeps a slider row and a segmented
row on the same 96px label column.

The endpoints are the part worth copying. `parameter-panel-demo` labels the Guidance slider ends "More
creative" and "More literal", and the Steps slider "Faster" and "More detail". The design spec section
4, under Parameters and pickers, is explicit: parameters speak human, never raw floats, and parameters
teach inline with one-line explanations under sliders. The demo's Guidance description does exactly
that, and it renders as `FieldRow`'s `hint`, so it is always-visible text rather than a tooltip.

Reset comes in two scopes and one component. `reset-affordance` at row scope sits in the row's `reset`
slot, and at group scope it sits in the panel header as reset-all. `parameter-panel.tsx` line 20
records that the group-scope version is the retrofit that gave `field-row` its `reset` slot in the
first place.

### The same settings, beside a prompt

<Example>
  <GenSettingsBarDemo />
</Example>

`gen-settings-bar.tsx` is 58 lines and a `role="toolbar"`, and the design spec section 4 opens its
Generation section with it: the model and params settings strip is universal, one primitive embedded
everywhere, in nodes, prompt bars and detail views. So the same values a `parameter-panel` stacks
vertically compress into a strip when the prompt is the focus.

Line 33 carries the contrast fix this repo keeps re-deriving: the bar paints `bg-muted/50` and sets
`text-foreground` plus a rebind of `--muted-foreground` on the same element. The comment gives the
measurement, `bg-muted/50` clears 4.5:1 by roughly 0.04 and only against that exact ancestor
background, which is too thin to inherit. The rebind is there because a composed child that sets
`text-muted-foreground` itself cannot be reached by a slot-level choice. `TOK-6` is the rule.

One honest gap is marked in the file at line 16: arrow-key roving tabindex for the toolbar pattern is
a TODO, and v1 ships Tab per item.

### The settings surface

<Example>
  <SettingsDialogDemo />
</Example>

`settings-dialog.tsx` pins three decisions with tests, and its own header comment states them. A row
is label plus description plus control, and `description` is a **required** string rather than an
optional nicety, on the reasoning that a toggle with no description is a setting nobody changes. A
destructive action renders as plain text in the control column and is not expressible as arbitrary
JSX: `destructiveAction` takes data and the component owns the rendering, so a filled button cannot
land beside benign toggles. Line 33 gives the second reason for that, `text-destructive` on a
translucent `bg-destructive/NN` measures 4.0:1 against the 4.5 minimum, so the text action carries no
tint at all. Line 119's `variant` is `dialog` or `full-page`, and the two share one row grid;
the variant changes the frame and whether search exists, nothing below `settings-dialog-panel`.

Two wizards, one step machine. `onboarding-wizard.tsx` line 18 says so outright: it is built on the
same `steps[]` plus controlled or uncontrolled `step` plus `onStepChange` contract as
`generation-wizard`, with Back, Skip and the primary in a footer row, because a second differently
shaped multi-step contract in one registry is how two components that look alike start behaving
differently.

Three departures are called out at their sites, and each one is a real difference in what the flow is
for. Skip survives onto the last step in onboarding, because an onboarding question is never a commit,
while the generation wizard's last step spends credits, so Skip has no meaning there and
`generation-wizard.tsx` line 243 renders it only when the step is not the last. Progress is a dot rail
rather than a titled stepper in onboarding, because step titles like "What do you do?" do not compress
into stepper labels and the useful fact is how many are left. And onboarding owns the answers, through
`answers`, `defaultAnswers` and `onAnswerChange`, where the generation wizard leaves each step's fields
to the caller.

The footer labels differ by what the last press does. `generation-wizard.tsx` lines 63 to 66 default to
"Back", "Skip", "Next" and a `finishLabel` of "Generate"; `onboarding-wizard.tsx` lines 115 to 118
default to the same first three and a `finishLabel` of "Finish setup". Back is `outline` and disabled
on the first step, Skip is `outline`, and the primary is `default`, one per footer.

## Panel, strip, or steps

The axis is what the reader is doing with the values, not how many there are.

- **`parameter-panel`** when the values are the task. Full label column, hints under the controls that
  need them, reset at both scopes. It is the surface a reader tunes.
- **`gen-settings-bar`** when the prompt is the task and the values are context. Same settings, one
  row, no hints. It is the surface a reader glances at and adjusts without leaving the composer.
- **`settings-dialog`** when the values are the account's rather than the run's, and every one of them
  needs a sentence of explanation. Its `full-page` variant is the same rows in a route.
- **`generation-wizard`** when a sequence ends in a commit that spends something. Its last step is a
  review, its preview pane shows what the current settings produce, and its finish label names the act.
- **`onboarding-wizard`** when a sequence collects answers and none of them commits. Every step is
  skippable, including the last.

A form that needs both a panel and a strip has one set of values in two places. Pick the surface the
reader is standing on and let the other read from the same state.

## Key decisions

- **The control gets its id from the row, never from the caller.** `field-row`'s render prop hands
  back `controlId` and `describedBy`, so an unlabelled control is a thing you have to work at.
- **A hint is text, not a tooltip.** It renders as `FieldRow`'s `hint` with a real id, reaching the
  control through `aria-describedby`. Parameters teach inline, per the design spec section 4.
- **Slider ends are words.** "More creative" and "More literal", not 0 and 20. The number can still
  show; the ends say what moving it does.
- **Every setting row carries a description.** `settings-dialog` types it as required, which is the
  strongest available statement that it is not decoration.
- **A destructive setting is text in the control column.** No filled button beside benign toggles, and
  no `text-destructive` on a `bg-destructive/NN` tint, which measures 4.0:1.
- **One step machine for both wizards.** New multi-step surfaces extend `steps[]` plus `step` plus
  `onStepChange` rather than growing a third shape.
- **Skip means something different in each wizard, and the components already encode it.** Do not add
  Skip to a committing last step, and do not remove it from a collecting one.
- **One primary per footer.** Back is `outline` and disabled at the start, Skip is `outline`, the
  primary is `default` and names the act.
- **Reset is one component at two scopes.** Row-level clears a value, group-level clears the panel.
  Both are `reset-affordance`, not a bespoke link.
- **Write logical properties on anything that holds a value.** `text-end` and `pe-*`, not `text-right`
  and `pr-*`, because the unit suffix moves under `dir="rtl"`.

## Rules

- Every control takes its id from `FieldRow`'s render prop. `children(controlId, describedBy)` is the
  API, and passing the ids is the caller's only job.
- A hint is rendered text with its own id, threaded to the control as `aria-describedby`. Do not put a
  parameter's meaning in a tooltip.
- Slider endpoints are plain language and describe the direction, per the design spec section 4:
  parameters speak human, never raw floats.
- Every row in a parameter surface composes `FieldRow`. A row that restyles the grid breaks the label
  column for every row beside it.
- Reset is `reset-affordance`, at row scope in `FieldRow`'s `reset` slot or at group scope in the
  panel header. Both states matter, modified and at-default.
- A settings row is label, description and control. `description` is required; a toggle without one is
  a setting nobody changes.
- A destructive setting is plain text in the control column, supplied as data through
  `destructiveAction`. Never a filled button, and never `text-destructive` on a `bg-destructive/NN`
  tint, which measures 4.0:1 against a 4.5:1 requirement per
  `docs/design-system/a11y-baseline.md`.
- `dialog` and `full-page` share one row grid. The variant changes the frame and whether search exists,
  so do not write a second row shape for the route version.
- Never write `text-muted-foreground` on `bg-muted`, `bg-accent` or `bg-secondary`. A strip that paints
  its own muted surface rebinds `--muted-foreground` on that element, the way `gen-settings-bar.tsx`
  line 33 does. `TOK-4`, `TOK-5` and `TOK-6` are the rules and `check:tokens` enforces them.
- A multi-step flow uses the shared machine: `steps[]`, controlled or uncontrolled `step`, and
  `onStepChange`. Do not introduce a third step contract.
- Skip appears on a committing wizard's non-last steps only, and on every step of a collecting one.
  `generation-wizard.tsx` line 243 and `onboarding-wizard.tsx` line 184 are the two shipped answers.
- One `default` button per footer, and it names the act: "Generate", "Finish setup". Back is `outline`
  and disabled on the first step.
- A committing wizard's last step reviews rather than asks. `generation-wizard-demo`'s third step reads
  the chosen model and style back and shows the total cost.
- The preview pane shows what the current settings produce, not a stock image. It is the cheap version
  of the design spec's preview-before-commit economics.
- Write logical direction properties on value-bearing controls: `text-end`, `pe-*`, `ms-*`. `UnitInput`
  lines 61 and 72 carry the reasons at the site.
- The commit that spends credits belongs to `run-button` and to **Patterns → Actions and
  confirmation**, along with the cost line beside it. A wizard hands the moment over.
- What any error says is **Content → Error messages**'. This page only says where it renders: on the
  field that is wrong, in the step the reader is standing on.

## Content notes

A field label names the value and takes no terminal period: "Volume", "Speed", "Guidance", "Quality",
"Steps", "Seed". A hint is a sentence and keeps its period, because it states something rather than
naming a slot. `field-row-demo` and `parameter-panel-demo` are both written that way.

Units are symbols in the control, not words in the label. `field-row-demo` writes "%" and "x" as the
`unit` prop rather than "Volume (percent)". The design spec section 4 asks for percent, second and
multiplier suffixes in compact inputs, which is what `UnitInput`'s trailing span is.

Segmented options are single words in sentence case: "Draft", "Standard", "High". Wizard step titles
are short noun phrases where they appear in a stepper ("Choose model", "Set style", "Review") and full
questions where they do not ("What do you do?"), which is exactly the reason the onboarding wizard
uses a dot rail instead.

Settings descriptions say what the setting does and, where there is one, the consequence with a
number: "Keep a copy of every prompt while you type, recoverable for 30 days." `CPY-1` asks the label
to say the thing, and `CPY-2` bans the marketing register, so a description states behaviour rather
than selling the feature.

This page owns where a form lives, how its rows are built, and how a sequence is stepped.
**Content → Error messages** owns what any failure says, **Patterns → Resource details** owns the view
up until editing becomes the task, and **Patterns → Actions & confirmation** owns the commit at the
end.
