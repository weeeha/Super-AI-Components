# Writing mechanics

**Content → Voice & principles** decides how a line sounds. This page decides how it is spelled:
case, marks, numerals, what happens when a string is longer than its box, and the words the
registry has already settled on. Nobody notices good mechanics, which is why they have to be
written down. Every count below was taken on 2026-09-09 over the 129 component files in
`apps/docs/registry/super-ai/`.

## Case

Sentence case everywhere a person reads words. Capitals are for proper nouns and model names.

The registry holds this without exception. Across every default label, title, text and placeholder,
zero strings are in Title Case, and zero literal `aria-label` values are either. The capitals that
do turn up mid-label are initialisms ("Ask AI", "AI tools") or product names supplied by the host,
which is where "Claude Opus 4.5" and "Veo 3.1" come from in the demos on the pages around this one.

Uppercase is a treatment applied in CSS and never typed into a string. Seven files carry the
micro-label pattern `tracking-wide uppercase` on a section heading: `ai-tools-menu.tsx:189`,
`detail-view-shell.tsx:183`, `filter-panel.tsx:302`, `generation-wizard.tsx:225`,
`pricing-table.tsx:181`, `section-header.tsx:61`, `usage-dashboard.tsx:187`. The eighth
`uppercase` in the tree is `diff-review.tsx:200`, a `first-letter:uppercase` that sentence-cases a
value coming from data. **Foundations → Typography** owns which rung that treatment sits on.

## Punctuation

The test is what the line does. A line that makes a statement closes with a period, even as a
fragment. A line that only names something never does.

The registry is measurably consistent here. Zero `title="..."` props in the registry end in a
period, and zero `description="..."` props omit one. A title that asks keeps its question mark:
`feedback.tsx:90` ships "Was this helpful?" and `feedback.tsx:97` ships "What went wrong?".

Four defaults end in `Label` and still carry a period, because each one is a whole statement:
`artifact-grid.tsx:224`, `chat-shell.tsx:272`, `inline-generate-popup.tsx:160`,
`settings-shell.tsx:255`. Read the line before reaching for the suffix.

One mark per job, as the three lines above show. The interpunct separates two facts of equal
weight and appears in 15 strings across 9 files, from `source-panel.tsx:124`'s quiet second line
to `render-queue.tsx:79`'s format summary. The em dash carries an aside or a consequence, and more
than a dozen strings use it that way, `rate-limit-banner.tsx:80` and `env-status.tsx:89` among
them. There are zero en dashes in the registry, so a numeric range has no settled form yet; pick
one and say which when the first range ships.

The ellipsis is one character. 23 strings across 15 files write it that way, including
`run-button.tsx:78` and `media-prompt-bar.tsx:74`. Exactly one string still spells it with three
periods, `skill-menu.tsx:71` (`searchPlaceholder = "Search skills..."`). Fix that one on touch.

## Numbers, units and the missing value

Three mechanics are visible in that one component. Numerals carry counts and identifiers, so the
seed reads as digits. `tabular-nums` locks the column so digits do not shuffle as a value updates;
49 class occurrences across 34 files use it, and **Foundations → Typography** owns the rung.
And a value that does not exist gets an em dash rather than a zero or a blank, because a zero is a
measurement and a blank is a bug. That placeholder ships at `stat-readout.tsx:37`,
`asset-library.tsx` lines 134, 136 and 465, `render-queue.tsx:153`, and `tts-composer.tsx:349`.
`track-list.tsx:40` records the reason inline and pairs the dash with an `sr-only` word, so a
listener hears something.

Units stay attached to their number in the string that owns them: "PDF · 2.4 MB" at
`source-panel.tsx:124`, "900 credits/min" as the rate form documented at `cost.tsx:40`.

A duration gets three forms in one file, and the split is worth copying.
`rate-limit-banner.tsx:87` formats the visible clock as digits. Line 97 spells the same wait in
words for the accessible name, because a clock read aloud is a sequence of numbers. Line 116
buckets it again, coarser, for the live region, so the announcement stays stable for a whole minute
instead of re-reading every second. **Foundations → Inclusive design** owns why the third one
exists.

## Truncation

Labels truncate. States and counts do not.

`truncate` appears 57 times across 36 files, always on a title or a name. `line-clamp-N` appears
exactly three times, and each one is a case where the extra lines still read:
`artifact-grid.tsx:173` at four lines for an artifact excerpt, `quote-reply.tsx:92` at two for a
quoted message, `source-cards.tsx:106` at two for a source snippet. `shrink-0` appears 146 times
across 68 files, which is how the badge, the status word, and the count survive at any width while
the label beside them gives way.

Choose by shape: `truncate` where the string must stay on one line, `line-clamp-N` where the
overflow is prose a reader would still want, and `shrink-0` on anything whose whole meaning is its
last character.

## The words already settled

Reach for a shipped vocabulary before coining one. Six sets cover most of what a surface needs to
say:

- **The six generation states.** `result-card.tsx:56` to `:63`: "Not started", "Queued",
  "Generating", "Result ready", "Generation failed", "Locked". This is the state contract from
  `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-06-10-super-ai-components-design.md` §6 in words.
- **The four provider states.** `env-status.tsx:75` to `:80`: "Ok", "Degraded", "Key invalid",
  "Not running", each with its own condition sentence and remedy at lines 82 to 99.
- **The four permission verbs.** `permission-prompt.tsx` lines 119 to 122: "Allow once",
  "Always allow", "Deny", "Edit first".
- **The five queue states.** `generation-queue.tsx:40`, with the label map at lines 88 to 94:
  "Queued", "Running", "Done", "Failed", "Cancelled".
- **The three paywall gates.** `paywall-message.tsx` lines 64 to 86, one title and one CTA each.
- **The disclaimer.** `disclaimer-note.tsx:15` ships one default sentence and three placements. A
  second wording for the same claim is a fork.

Two habits worth copying. A state word is always visible text beside its colour, never the colour
alone: `env-status.tsx:74` records that its badge exists so the row is readable without hue.
And a status word never grades the reader, which is **Content → Voice & principles**' rule and the
reason "Not helpful" at `feedback.tsx:92` is phrased about the answer.

## Rules

- Sentence case for every title, label, button, tab, menu item and chip. Proper nouns and model
  names are the only capitals. Zero registry strings are Title Cased; keep it at zero.
- Uppercase is a CSS treatment, applied by seven files as `tracking-wide uppercase`. Never type it
  into a string.
- No terminal period on a title or a label that only names something. A line that makes its own
  statement closes with a period even as a fragment. A question keeps its question mark.
- One mark per job: the interpunct for two facts of equal weight, the em dash for an aside. There
  are no en dashes in the registry, so the first numeric range sets the convention.
- The ellipsis is one character. `skill-menu.tsx:71` is the last string spelling it with three
  periods.
- A count or an identifier is a numeral. A number belonging to a phrase stays a word.
- A column of numbers takes `tabular-nums`.
- A missing value renders an em dash with an `sr-only` word beside it, per `track-list.tsx:40`.
  Never a zero, never a blank.
- Labels truncate; states, counts and badges take `shrink-0` and survive whole. `line-clamp-N` only
  where the clipped lines were prose worth reading.
- Reuse a settled vocabulary before writing a synonym. The six sets above are the first place to
  look.
- Strings are English, per D22 in `docs/design-system/decisions.md`.

This page owns how the interface is written. **Content → Voice & principles** owns how it sounds:
that page when the question is which word, this one when the question is which mark.
