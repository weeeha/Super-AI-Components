# Voice & principles

The interface reports and then stops. It says what happened and what the reader can do next, in that
order, and it never congratulates anyone for arriving. Every claim below describes copy this
registry already ships: all of it was re-measured on 2026-09-09 across the 129 component files in
`apps/docs/registry/super-ai/`, and the strings quoted are the defaults a consumer gets by deciding
nothing. Two records in `packages/ds-rules/rules/core.json` carry parts of it, `CPY-1` and `CPY-2`.
Only the first gates: `check:tokens` runs `rulecheck.mjs --severity blocker`
(`apps/docs/package.json:12`), which filters to blocker records and so never evaluates `CPY-2` at
all. A bare `node packages/ds-rules/rulecheck.mjs` reports it.

## Front-load the verdict

The first line is the outcome. Anything qualifying it goes underneath, where a reader who stopped
at the title has already been told the thing that changes what they do.

Both titles above are `safety-block.tsx`'s own per-variant copy (lines 28 to 37). "Request blocked"
and "Response withheld" are two different events, so they get two different words, and the body
underneath each says which one happened to the work: one request never left, the other ran and
produced something the reader is not getting. The policy name sits below both, because a block with
no named source reads as a bug.

`paywall-message.tsx` holds the same shape across three gates (lines 64 to 86). The titles name the
fact: "This model is not on your plan", "You are out of credits", "This feature is not on your plan".
All three then share one description, "The run was not started, so nothing was charged.", which is
the sentence a reader actually needs from a paywall.

## Verbs, and the object they act on

A control's label is a verb plus the thing the verb touches, so the label predicts the next screen.
That is `CPY-1`, a blocker in `packages/ds-rules/rules/core.json` enforced by `check:tokens` over
registry sources.

The defaults hold it. `workspace-switcher.tsx:80` ships "Create workspace", `feedback.tsx:99` ships
"Send feedback", `permission-prompt.tsx` lines 119 to 122 ship "Allow once", "Always allow", "Deny"
and "Edit first", and `paywall-message.tsx`'s three CTAs each name what upgrading buys rather than
saying "Upgrade": "Upgrade to use this model", "Upgrade for more credits", "Upgrade to unlock it".

`empty-state.tsx` makes the rule structural instead of hoping for it. Lines 26 to 31 record that the
component has no `ctaLabel` prop on purpose, so a caller cannot reach for a generic label by
accident; the CTA is caller-supplied because only the caller knows the verb of the surface the empty
state is standing in for.

Two defaults do fall through, and they fall through the gate as well. `CPY-1` greps for
`Get Started|Learn More` with no case-insensitive flag, so `recommendation-card.tsx:79`
(`commitLabel = "Get started"`) and `promo-card.tsx:178` (the `ctaLabel` fallback "Learn more") both
pass CI while being exactly what the rule is about. Both are open gaps; copying either one forward
spreads a label that predicts nothing.

## Say the number

Where a quantity exists, the copy carries it. A reader who is blocked wants to know how blocked, and
"soon" answers nothing.

`rate-limit-banner.tsx` renders a live countdown rather than a word, and its two causes carry
different prefixes because they are different facts: "Resets in" for the reader's own plan limit,
"Estimated wait" for provider capacity (lines 51 to 84). When no number is available it says so,
"No reset time reported yet." on one branch and a matching line on the other, instead of inventing
an estimate. The resource line in the demo above is the host's, and it counts: "Image generations ·
50 of 50 used today".

The counting habit runs through the shells. `artifact-shell.tsx` lines 276 to 282 build a summary
that says the whole number when nothing is filtered and "N of M artifacts" when something is, which
is what separates a filtered list from an empty one. `generation-queue`'s demo heads its queue
"Generating 4 images". **Foundations → Data Visualization** owns what a chart does with the same
numbers.

## No cheerleading

`CPY-2` in `packages/ds-rules/rules/core.json` names the banned marketing register and the vocabulary
that carries it; read the record for the list. Its severity is `warning` for a reason the record
states in full: this repo's gate family uses one of those words as the literal mechanism a plan
upgrade performs, and a word-boundary pattern cannot separate that sense from the boast. So the
register is a judgement you make, and CI will not make it for you.

Praise is the other half, and it has one shipped violation. Exactly one exclamation mark reaches a
reader from a registry default: `feedback.tsx:100`, `submittedLabel = "Thanks for the feedback!"`.
That is the whole set, measured across all 129 files. Nothing else in the registry thanks, praises,
or exclaims.

## Failures and empty regions carry more weight than the happy path

A success line is glanced at once. A refusal or a dead end is read closely, because the reader is
stuck and needs the way out. That is why the registry spends its longest strings there:
`connection-manager.tsx` gives every one of its nine connection conditions a two-part label and a
remedy (lines 104 to 149), and `env-status.tsx` does the same for four (lines 82 to 99).

**Content → Error messages** owns what a failure says and **Content → Empty states** owns what a
zero state says. This page owns the register both of them speak in.

## Which voice, by situation

- **A refusal on policy.** The event first, the named policy second, the compliant path last.
  `safety-block.tsx`'s two variants are the shape, and its `alternatives` prop exists so a refusal
  always has an exit.
- **A gate that money or a plan can open.** Name the gate, say that nothing was charged, and hold
  the work. `paywall-message.tsx` requires the prompt verbatim so upgrading resumes rather than
  retypes.
- **A temporary limit.** Say which constraint fired and how long it lasts.
  `rate-limit-banner.tsx` keeps its two causes apart because sending someone to buy an upgrade when
  the model is merely busy fixes nothing.
- **A run in flight.** A present participle and nothing else: "Generating…" (`run-button.tsx:79`),
  "Estimating cost…" (`run-button.tsx:78`).
- **A completed action.** A past-tense verb phrase about the event. `result-card.tsx:56` to `:63`
  holds the six-state set, and its success reads "Result ready" rather than a compliment.
- **A status word.** Reuse a shipped one before coining a synonym. `env-status.tsx` lines 75 to 80
  and `result-card.tsx` lines 56 to 63 are the two vocabularies to check first.
- **A per-item failure.** Name what failed and put the retry beside it. `source-panel.tsx:264`
  names the source inside the retry control's own accessible name.
- **A disclaimer.** One sentence, no hedging around it. `disclaimer-note.tsx:15` ships
  "AI can make mistakes. Check important info." as the default and every surface reuses it.
- **An error.** **Content → Error messages**.
- **An empty or zero state.** **Content → Empty states**.

## Rules

- The first line is the outcome. Qualifiers go under it, never inside it.
- A label is a verb plus its object, so it predicts the next screen. `CPY-1` in
  `packages/ds-rules/rules/core.json` is the record; its grep is case-sensitive, so sentence-case
  generic labels are your responsibility rather than CI's.
- Never add a `ctaLabel`-style default that makes a generic label the easy path. `empty-state.tsx`
  omits the prop for this reason.
- Say the number wherever one exists, and say that there is no number when there is not. Never
  "soon", never "some".
- No praise and no exclamation marks. One ships today, at `feedback.tsx:100`. Fix it on touch.
- No marketing register, per `CPY-2`. That record is a warning, so `check:tokens` never evaluates
  it and the judgement is yours; the one vocabulary item this repo uses literally is documented in
  the record itself.
- Two states that need different remedies get different words. A key the provider rejected and a
  provider that never answered send the reader to different places, which is why
  `connection-manager.tsx` writes four distinct hosted remedies instead of one.
- Reuse a shipped status word before inventing one.
- Strings are English and hardcoded, per D22 in `docs/design-system/decisions.md`. What that costs a
  consumer is **Content → Inclusive writing**.

This page decides how the interface sounds. **Content → Writing mechanics** decides how it is
spelled: case, punctuation, numerals, and the words already settled.
